Twenty funds · round-level evidence

Who funded what in crypto, one round at a time

Every investment here exists because a fund appeared in a funding round, not because it put itself on a portfolio page. The dataset was built in one pass over every project page on the source, then inverted to fund. That ordering is the whole point: fund pages show ten rows and quietly drop the failures.

01

Fund pages measure survivors, so the round is the unit

Two measured properties disqualify fund pages as a primary source. First, a display limit: the source's fund page renders a fixed handful of rounds, and pagination redirects back to page one. Second, survivorship bias: a fund maintaining its own portfolio page has no reason to keep written-off companies listed.

Both effects point the same way — scraping per fund measures the winners and calls it a portfolio. So this dataset reads every project page instead, parses all investors per round, and only then inverts the index to fund.
